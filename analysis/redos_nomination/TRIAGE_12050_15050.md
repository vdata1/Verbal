# ReDoS queue triage — window 12050–15050

**Prepared:** 2026-08-03 · **Input:** `results/redos_queue_12050_15050.json` (239 nominations,
`status: deferred`) · **Method:** static + recorded-artifact only, plus one unloaded spot-check.
Nothing here depends on how loaded the box was, which is the point — the queue's own caveat says
its milliseconds are inflated and cannot be trusted.

> **Headline: the queue is mostly load artifacts, and it yields no engine-specific finding.**
> 239 rows collapse to 26 regexes, of which **13 are structurally incapable of superlinear
> backtracking** yet every one recorded a 20 s timeout. Only two are exponential-capable, and
> **both have now been resolved unloaded (§4, §4b): each is real ReDoS in the regex itself, and
> neither is an engine differential.** A confirm run should be driven from the 26-regex list, not
> the 239 rows, and on this window its engine-specific yield is zero.

---

## 1. Rows are not findings: 239 → 26 (9.2× amplification)

One regex, `regex_14648`, is **86 of the 239 rows**. Confirming per-row pays the serial cost nine
times over for no additional information. Worst-case serial confirm drops from **239 min to
26 min** (26 regexes × 3 engines × 20 s) by keying on the regex.

## 2. Three buckets

Classified on a *sound* necessary condition rather than a shape guess: superlinear backtracking
requires the engine to have a choice about how to divide the input, which needs at least two
unbounded quantifiers, or one nested under another / under an alternation. A pattern with ≤1
unbounded quantifier and no quantified group has exactly one way to match any prefix and **cannot**
be superlinear.

| bucket | regexes | rows |
|---|---:|---:|
| EXPONENTIAL-capable | 2 | 87 |
| polynomial-possible (conservative) | 11 | 73 |
| **cannot backtrack at all** | **13** | **79** |

The "cannot backtrack" bucket contains, verbatim: `[㐀-龿]`, `\s[/~]`, `^cms.*`,
`(expired|does not exist)`, `alimama\.(com|net)`, `utc|_.|^.`, `\b[a-f0-9]{64}\b`,
`--.*\n`, `\\\\([0-9]+)`, `^(5|5[1-5]|2|22|…|2720)$`, `\&(?!\w{1,15};)`,
``(?<!\[)`([A-Za-z_0-9]+)` ``, `struct hoedown_renderer \{(.*?)\}`.

A single character class cannot take 20 seconds. The clearest case is `regex_14049`
`[㐀-龿]` on the input `"鷽"` — **one character** — recorded as a node timeout.

## 3. The timeout pattern proves it independently

Which engines timed out, per bucket:

| bucket | timed-out engines | rows where **all three** timed out |
|---|---|---:|
| cannot backtrack | node 28 / deno 26 / bun 25 | **0 / 79** |
| polynomial-possible | bun 29 / node 24 / deno 20 | **0 / 73** |
| EXPONENTIAL-capable | 41 none, **35 node+deno together**, 10 node, 1 all three | 1 / 87 |

In the two lower buckets the timeouts are spread **uniformly across engines and never hit more
than one engine at a time**. That is the signature of random process starvation: whichever engine
happened to be scheduled unluckily under a saturated pool. A genuine regex problem would hit all
three engines (a property of the pattern) or the same engine consistently (a property of the
engine). Neither occurs.

The exponential bucket looks completely different — 35 rows where **node and deno time out
together while bun does not**. node and deno are both V8, so that is a real V8-vs-JSC split, not
noise. It is the only structured signal in the queue.

## 4. Spot-check: `regex_14648` re-run unloaded

`^(?:[^`"[]+|`[^`]*`|"[^"]*")* AS\s+` — alternation under `*`, the textbook exponential shape.
Took the row where node+deno timed out and bun did not (`match`, flags `g`, 65-char input), and
re-ran it pinned to idle cores with a 300 s budget:

| engine | pool (loaded) | **unloaded** | value |
|---|---|---|---|
| bun | 15 115 ms | **3 957 ms** | `NULL` |
| node | timeout > 20 000 ms | **5 840 ms** | `NULL` |
| deno | timeout > 20 000 ms | **5 763 ms** | `NULL` |

Three things follow:

1. **All three engines agree on the value.** No F004-style "fast wrong answer" — bun's 15 s in the
   pool was not a step-cap bailout, it was a loaded run of a genuinely expensive match. The
   value-differential idea does not extend F004 here.
2. **Load inflated the readings 3–4×** and turned two finishing runs into timeouts. This is a
   direct measurement of the effect the queue caveat warns about.
3. **It is a real ReDoS regex but not an engine differential.** ~4–6 s on a 65-character input is
   badly superlinear, so it clears `slow_ms` (1000) easily — but the engine ratio is
   5 840 / 3 957 ≈ **1.5×**, far under the `engine_ratio` gate of 10. Per `_confirm_redos` it
   would be recorded and *not* flagged `engine_specific`: a property of the regex, not of an
   engine, and therefore not the kind of result this project reports.

## 4b. Spot-check: `regex_14841` — the queue's only non-timeout nomination

`(:?0x(?:(?:\d|[abcdefABCDEF]){0,2})+) +in +(:?0x(?:(?:\d|[abcdefABCDEF]){0,2})+)`

The one row in the whole queue with a real reading and no timeout (`replaceAll`, `g`, 71-char
input, bun 1741 ms under load). `(X{0,2})+` over a possibly-empty body is exponential-capable,
so this was the strongest remaining candidate. (`(:?` is a typo for `(?:` in the corpus regex —
those are capturing groups.)

Re-run unloaded on idle cores, plus a length ladder over the pumpable hex run:

| k (hex chars) | node | bun | deno |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 16 | 1 ms | 6 ms | 0 ms |
| 20 | 4 ms | 36 ms | 5 ms |
| 24 | 30 ms | 243 ms | 28 ms |
| 28 | 204 ms | 1 639 ms | 198 ms |
| *real input (71 ch)* | *18 ms* | *151 ms* | *20 ms* |

**It is genuinely exponential, and the base is the same in every engine.** Fitting the last two
rungs: node **1.615**, bun **1.612**, deno ≈1.63 — all φ (1.618). That is exactly right for
`(X{0,2})+`: each repetition consumes one or two characters, so the number of partitions of an
n-character run is Fibonacci, giving φⁿ. The measurement agreeing with the closed form is good
evidence the ladder is measuring what it claims to.

**All three engines return the identical output string**, so there is no value discrepancy.

**Not an engine differential.** bun's constant is ~8× worse than V8 (8.0× at k=28, 8.4× on the
real input) — under the `engine_ratio` gate of 10 — but more importantly the *base is identical*,
so this is one algorithm with different constants, not a difference in algorithmic class. That is
the same trap [`../HANDOFF_redos_timing.md`](../HANDOFF_redos_timing.md) R5 retracted: a constant
factor between two engines that are both exponential at the same base is not a finding about an
engine. The pool's 1741 ms was ~11× inflated against the 151 ms unloaded.

## 5. What this means for the confirm run

- **Drive it from the 26-regex list**, not the 239 rows.
- **Expected yield is low.** 13 regexes are provably not ReDoS; the 11 "polynomial-possible" ones
  are a conservative bucket (e.g. `rgb\(\s*(\d+(?:\.\d+)?)\%…` is separated by literals and almost
  certainly fine) and show the same random-starvation signature. The only two exponential-capable
  regexes, `regex_14648` and `regex_14841`, are both spot-checked below.
- **Both exponential candidates are now resolved** (§4, §4b) and neither is an engine
  differential: `regex_14648` is real ReDoS at a 1.5× ratio, `regex_14841` is real ReDoS at φ in
  *all three* engines with an 8× constant. **The 239-nomination queue yields zero engine-specific
  ReDoS findings.** Both regexes are genuine ReDoS in their own right — worth noting as properties
  of the corpus regexes, but not the differential result this project reports.
- What remains for a confirm run is therefore only the 11 "polynomial-possible" regexes, which
  show the same random-starvation signature as the provably-safe bucket and are expected to drop.

## 6. Actionable: the nomination criterion admits starvation

A row is nominated when *any single engine* exceeds the harness timeout under pool load. On a
saturated box that fires on process scheduling, which is why a one-character regex on a
one-character input is in the queue.

A **free static pre-filter** would have removed 13 regexes / 79 rows before they were ever queued:
*if the pattern has ≤1 unbounded quantifier and no quantified group, it cannot be superlinear —
never nominate it.* That is sound (it can only reject true negatives) and costs no measurement.

A second cheap tightening: require **≥2 engines** to exceed the budget, or re-check a single-engine
timeout once before queuing. On this window either rule alone removes essentially all 152 rows in
the two lower buckets.

### Caveats

- The bucket rule is sound in the "cannot backtrack" direction only. "polynomial-possible" is
  deliberately over-inclusive and should not be read as suspicion.
- The spot-check covers one row of one regex. It settles that regex's engine-ratio question on
  that input; it does not settle the other 25.
- Everything about *values* here is load-independent. The unloaded timings were taken on cores
  0–3 while a run held 8–47, so they are upper bounds, which only strengthens the conclusion that
  the pool figures were inflated.
